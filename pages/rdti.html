What is it?

The R&D Tax Incentive explained.

The Research and Development (R&D) Tax Incentive is an Australian Government program administered jointly by AusIndustry (Department of Industry, Science and Resources) and the Australian Taxation Office (ATO).

The program encourages companies to conduct R&D activities in Australia by providing a tax offset, or additional deductible expenditure, equal to a percentage of your eligible R&D expenditure, depending on your tax position.

This is not a deduction. It is an offset against your tax payable, or a direct cash refund to your bank account. Over $2.9 billion is awarded annually, and it is not competitive. If you are eligible, you are entitled to it.

Key difference from tax deductions

A deduction reduces your taxable income. An offset reduces your tax payable dollar-for-dollar. For companies with turnover under $20M, any unused offset is paid to you as a cash refund, regardless of profit.

A startup spending $500,000 on eligible R&D can receive up to $217,500 in cash (indicative), even with zero revenue.

⚠️ Under and overclaiming are both common

Self-lodging businesses typically miss 20-40% of eligible activities. At the same time, many businesses already claiming are overclaiming without realising it, which carries real risk under an ATO or AusIndustry review. GrantReady's specialist review identifies both.

Who administers the program?

AusIndustry handles R&D registration, while the ATO processes the claim as part of your income tax return. It is a self-assessment program: both agencies may review or audit your submission at any time.

Before getting swept away with the thought of thousands of dollars cash-back for your business, you need to examine eligibility carefully. There are three tests, and you must satisfy all three. GrantReady can assess all of these at no charge.

01

Applicant Eligibility

Your business structure and ownership must satisfy the program requirements.

  • Must be an incorporated company, Pty Ltd or Ltd (Australian, or foreign with an Australian permanent establishment)
  • R&D activities must generally be conducted in Australia, though prior approval can allow limited overseas activity
  • You must own the R&D results, or have the right to access them
  • Exempt entities (trusts, partnerships, sole traders) do not qualify directly, there are limited exceptions, speak to us to discuss
02

Activity Eligibility

A distinction must be made between core and supporting R&D activities, both must be reported separately.

  • Core R&D: experimental activities whose outcomes cannot be known in advance
  • Supporting R&D: activities directly related to core R&D activities
  • Activities must generate new knowledge using scientific method
  • Scientific method: hypothesis → experiment → observation and evaluation → conclusion
  • You must retain evidence that a knowledge gap existed at the time of activity
03

Expenditure Eligibility

The R&D Tax Incentive is not subject to an expenditure cap, but there are minimums and thresholds.

  • Minimum $20,000 eligible R&D expenditure per income year
  • Eligible costs include: wages, contractor payments, overhead directly attributable to R&D, plant and equipment
  • No cap on eligible R&D expenditure up to $150 million
  • Above $150M in R&D expenditure, a non-refundable offset rate applies to the excess

Companies with turnover <$20M
43.5%

The refundable R&D tax offset is 18.5 percentage points above the claimant's company tax rate, equating to 43.5%. Once tax liability reaches zero, companies receive a cash refund for any unused offset amount.

Companies with turnover >$20M
8.5-16.5%

Larger companies face a two-tier intensity measure. R&D expenditure between 0-2% R&D intensity receives 8.5% above company tax rate; anything above 2% receives 16.5%. No cash refund, excess offsets carry forward to future years.

While GrantReady handles the hard work of preparing and lodging your claim, a strong claim still depends on your company maintaining good record keeping throughout the financial year. The better your records, the stronger and more defensible your claim.

Applicants must keep adequate records to demonstrate to both AusIndustry and the ATO that:

R&D activities have been undertaken

  • Records must show that the claimed activities took place and met the definition of core or supporting R&D
  • Evidence of experimental activities, including use of scientific method to generate new knowledge and overcome uncertainty
  • Documents showing a knowledge gap existed at the time: literature reviews, patent searches, scientific or technology reviews, trade journals

Eligible expenditure was incurred

  • Timesheets or summaries of individual employees' total hours charged to the R&D project
  • Salary and wages, superannuation, payroll tax, workers' compensation
  • Contractor payments, travel expenses, cost of trials, R&D plant and equipment
  • General administration overheads attributable to R&D

All legislative requirements are satisfied

  • Records must demonstrate that the activities and expenditure meet all eligibility criteria under the program
  • GrantReady conducts structured interviews with your technical team to build a compliant documentation package
